09 Section: One decision

10 One decision per dashboard

Story: Maria's supplier just raised the price of chicken wings. Her decision: do we raise the menu price?

Every dashboard I build starts with four things. The decision. The number it needs. Who decides. By when.

For Maria: raise the chicken wing price? The number is chicken wing margin after this week's supplier price. The general manager decides. By Friday.

Ask the room: Now take your decision. What one number would settle it? Who in your business makes the call? Take ten seconds.

Ask two people to share. Repeat each answer back in the four parts: decision, number, who, when.

If a chart on a screen has no decision attached, cut it.

Transition: Gartner has a simple way to describe what that screen should do.

11 Ask, Act, Tell

Gartner describes three jobs for a useful screen. Ask, Act, Tell.

Ask: what happened? Act: what do we do about it? Tell: who needs to know?

Where the three overlap is the one decision you just picked.

We add a fourth, Stop. When a number crosses a line the owner set, the screen stops and a person decides.

Transition: Here is Maria's chicken wing decision on one screen.

12 One dashboard: Ask, Act, Tell, Stop

Here is Maria's screen for one decision: the chicken wing price. Sample numbers.

Ask. Chicken wing margin was 68 percent last month. This week it is 63 percent. The supplier raised a 10 piece order from 3.90 to 4.40. That is 13 percent.

Act. The screen proposes one action. Raise chicken wings from 12 dollars to 12.75, which brings margin back to 65 percent. The general manager approves or holds it, by Friday.

Tell. On Monday Maria gets one sentence, written from the same numbers.

Stop. Maria set two lines. Margin below 60 percent, or a price change over 10 percent, and the screen stops and asks her.

Ask the room: Picture your decision on a screen like this. What would the Ask box show?

Transition: That solved Maria's first problem. Her second problem was harder to see.

13 Section: Guests who drift away

Story: Joe came to Maria's every Thursday for trivia. Four years. Same seat, same beer.

14 Every scan is a row of data

Maria had no way to see it. Joe paid cash. He was a familiar face with no record.

A loyalty card changes that. Every time a guest scans, the venue records who they are, when they came, and how often.

The guest agreed to join, and the date they agreed is stored. No payment card details are collected.

That is a guest list the venue owns. A marketing team pays for this kind of data. Maria builds it at the bar.

17 Section: Keeping it safe

Now Maria has her sales, her costs and her guest list in one place. That makes it valuable.

18 A person approves the message

The system drafts the win-back message to Joe. Maria reads it and approves it before it goes out.

Every approval is logged with the time. That log is her record of what she decided, and when.

Remember the third problem from the start: data going into public tools. Maria's data stays in her own system.
